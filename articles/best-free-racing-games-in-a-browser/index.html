The best free racing and driving games you can play in a browser

16 October 2026 · 5 min read

PLAYPILE has over a dozen driving games, and they are not the same game in different paint. This list is for anyone who wants to get behind a wheel without installing anything, whether you have an evening for a full racing season or ten minutes for some traffic dodging. They run roughly from the most serious to the most silly.

APEX

APEX is the deepest racing game on the site. It has 33 circuits (the 24 on the 2026 calendar plus nine former venues such as Imola, Mugello and Hockenheim), twelve invented teams, five tyre compounds and four kinds of weather. Every car on the grid uses the same physics as yours, and the rivals get no catch-up help. Modes run from a flying-lap Time Attack against your own ghost to a League season of up to 22 rounds, saved after every session.

It is on this list because it treats racing seriously: an eight-speed manual gearbox, a tow you have to earn, DRS, pit stops you drive into yourself, and damage that can take a wheel off. The catch is that it is a keyboard game, with no controller or touch support. Tip: the tow works within 55 m of the car ahead, but the same disturbed air costs you downforce, so make your pass on the straight, not in the corner behind him.

NASCAR

NASCAR does for ovals what APEX does for road courses. There are twenty tracks, from Daytona and Talladega to Bristol and Martinsville, with up to forty cars on a rolling start. Cautions come out for wrecks and stalled cars, the field bunches up, and a big lead can vanish in one yellow. There are single races, endurance races of up to 500 laps and seasons of up to 18 rounds.

Pick this one if you like strategy inside a race: when to pit, how hard to use the tyres, and who to draft with. Like APEX, it is played on a keyboard only. Tip: pit under yellow whenever you can. Everyone else does, because a stop at pace speed costs almost nothing, and your box is the one that matches your starting position on the grid.

RUSH HOUR

RUSH HOUR is pure arcade speed. The throttle is always on, and you weave through traffic down one 36 km road split into ten stages, each ending at a checkpoint that tops up your clock. The stages change as you go, from a freight line full of trucks to roadworks, oncoming traffic and a final night-time gauntlet in the rain.

It is the easiest game here to pick up, and it works on a phone too: hold either side of the screen to steer, with on-screen brake and nitro buttons. Tip: the car will steer into almost any gap, so speed is the real danger. Brake a little before blind bends, where traffic bunches up.

TOUGE DRIFT

TOUGE DRIFT is a top-down drifting game. You slide an 80s hatchback with pop-up headlights down a Japanese mountain pass at dusk, with eurobeat playing. The road is built as you drive, so every run is a new mountain. There is an eighty-second downhill race against three rivals, a 45-second Time Attack with checkpoints that add time, and a Free Run with no clock.

It fits this list for anyone who cares more about style than finishing position. Drifts build a multiplier up to x10, and sliding close to the rail doubles the points. On a phone the gas is automatic and you steer by holding each half of the screen, but there is no brake or nitro on touch. Tip: start your next drift within 2.2 seconds of banking the last one and you keep the multiplier, so flick straight from one slide into the next on S-bends.

KART CLASH

KART CLASH is a kart battle rather than a race. You and five bots drive around a studded toy-plastic arena for two minutes, pick up items from ? boxes and try to knock out as many karts as you can. Knocked-out karts come back, so it is about scoring, not surviving. The four items are a homing rocket, a gumball gun, pop mines and a short-range shockwave.

It is the best pick here for a quick, noisy round. It has a thumb stick and a fire button on phones. Tip: you can only score with items, so plan a route that takes you through the next ? box as soon as you have used one.

DEMOLITION DERBY

DEMOLITION DERBY puts eight cars in a walled dirt bowl, and the last one still running wins. Where you hit matters more than how often: every car has front, rear, side and engine damage, and you can watch panels fall off and engines go from grey smoke to black smoke to fire. After 75 seconds a ring of fire closes in, so no round drags on. You earn coins to buy four more cars (up to a school bus) and open two more arenas, a muddy farm field and a figure-eight.

The seven computer drivers each have habits: some are bullies, one runs away and one hunts the weakest car. Tip: lead with your nose. Your front bumper takes less than half damage and your doors take a quarter more, so turn to face anything coming at you and save boost for your own T-bone.

STUNT CITY

STUNT CITY has no track at all. You drive a chrome car around a vaporwave stunt park full of ramps, loops and rings, flipping, spinning and barrel-rolling in the air for cash. Eight challenges, from a sunset sprint to a big-air jump and a final stunt show, teach you what the car can do, and the garage sells engine and nitro upgrades. It has on-screen buttons on phones.

It is here for players who would rather mess about than race. Tip: let go of the flip and spin keys a moment before you land so the car settles wheels-down. A wipe-out loses your combo.

PIZZA PANIC

PIZZA PANIC is the lightest game on the list. You ride a scooter through a top-down city grid and throw pizzas at the glowing doors of the people who ordered them, without stopping. Each shift lasts 80 seconds, every delivery adds 5 seconds, and you need to hit a cash target to move on. From the second shift, rival scooters try to ram pizzas off your rack. Earnings buy upgrades between shifts.

It is a driving game where steering well pays the bills, and it plays on a phone with a floating thumb stick and a tap to throw. Tip: pizzas cool from the moment you pick them up, and a cold one pays only about a third of the tip, so plan a loop that reaches every door while the food is still hot.

If you want a proper racing career, start with APEX or NASCAR and set aside an evening, and have a keyboard ready. For something quick, RUSH HOUR and KART CLASH are the fastest way into the action, and both work on a phone. Everything saves in your browser, so you can close the tab and pick up where you left off.
