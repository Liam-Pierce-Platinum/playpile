How to play

PAPER LAND is a territory game in which everything is made of cut paper on a sheet of graph paper. You are a folded-paper token, and you start on a small patch of paper in your colour. The goal is to cover a set share of the board with your own paper.

Your token moves towards the mouse cursor. You can also steer in eight directions with W A S D or the arrow keys, or on a phone by dragging anywhere on the screen.

When you leave your land, you lay a lighter paper strip behind you. Steer back into your land and everything inside the loop is cut out and becomes yours. While the strip is out, you are vulnerable: if a rival crosses your strip, you are snipped off the sheet, and if you touch your own strip, you tear. Either way the level starts again.

Tips and strategy

Small, safe bites. A short trip out and back is hard to catch. Big loops claim more land but leave a long strip out for rivals to cross.

Watch the rivals, not just your path. If a rival is heading for your strip, turn for home straight away.

Snip them back. Rivals are vulnerable in exactly the same way. If one has a long strip out, cross it to cut them off the sheet.

Do not cross your own line. When you turn for home, make a wide U-turn rather than a sharp one so you do not clip your own strip.

Mind the goal. The share you need is shown during the level. Once you are close, play safe.

What's in it

A level-by-level game where each level adds another rival, makes the rivals faster and smarter, and raises the land goal, from 20% of the board up to 60%. Each level you clear unlocks a new paper colour, which you pick on the menu or result card. Your progress is saved.

The board is one big sheet of graph paper on a kraft table, and each player's land is a cut sheet with its own pattern, wobbly hand-cut edges and soft shadows. Sounds are paper rustles, snips and crumples.

Questions

Do I need an account? No. Your levels and paper colours are saved in your browser.

Is it violent? No. Caught tokens are just snipped off the paper.

Why did I lose without anyone touching me? You probably touched your own strip, which tears it.

Does it flash? No.
