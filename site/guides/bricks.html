How to play

BRICKS is breakout. A wall of six rows and ten columns sits at the top of the screen, with a few gaps punched through it at random. You start with three balls. Click, tap or press Space to launch the ball off your paddle, then keep it in play while it chips the wall away. Clear every brick and a new wall appears, the wall number goes up, and your paddle is restored to full width.

Ordinary bricks take one hit and score 10. From the second wall onwards, one complete row is steel: it takes two hits, scores 25, and moves down a row on each new wall, wrapping back to the top, so the shot that opened the last wall will not open this one. Clearing a wall is worth a further 100.

The catch is the paddle. It shrinks steadily while a wall is in play, from full width to well under half of it in about 25 seconds, so a slow wall becomes a hard one. Losing a ball costs a life; when the last one goes, the run is over and your score goes to the board.

Tips and strategy

  • Aim with the paddle, not the timing. The angle the ball leaves at depends only on where it hits the paddle. Dead centre sends it straight up; the very edge sends it out at about sixty degrees from vertical.
  • Get through a gap early. Each wall has a few holes. A ball that gets above the wall bounces between the top and the bricks, scoring without you having to touch it.
  • Work fast on each wall. The shrinking paddle only resets when the wall is cleared. Losing a ball tops the paddle back up to four-fifths of full width if it had shrunk below that, but that is an expensive way to get it back.
  • Expect the ball to get quicker. It speeds up slightly on every paddle hit, and each new wall serves it a little faster than the last.
  • Judge every drop. Roughly one broken brick in eight releases a drop that falls straight down, and you must catch it on the paddle. Going for one can mean leaving the ball.
  • Multi is the one to chase. It splits up to two balls in play into two more each, and you only lose a life when every ball is gone.
  • Mouse and keys both work. Whichever you touched last drives the paddle. On the keys it moves at a fixed speed, which is steadier for small corrections; the mouse is quicker for long moves.

What's in it

Endless walls, each freshly laid out. Four kinds of drop, each marked with its letter:

  • W – Wide: adds width to the paddle, up to one and a half times full size.
  • M – Multi: splits the balls in play into extra balls. It and Wide are the most common.
  • S – Slow: takes about a quarter off the speed of every ball.
  • L – Life: an extra ball in reserve, plus 50 points.

Questions

Does it save my score?

Yes, on this device. The home screen shows a top-ten table, and a run that makes it asks for three initials. Your best score also appears in the bar along the top while you play.

Can I play it on a phone?

Yes. The paddle follows your finger as you drag, and a tap launches the ball.

How does it get harder?

Every wall serves the ball faster, the steel row moves to a new position, and within each wall the paddle shrinks the longer you take.

What happens if I lose focus?

The game pauses itself and lets go of any held keys, so the paddle will not drift off on its own. Click to carry on, or press P to pause by hand.
