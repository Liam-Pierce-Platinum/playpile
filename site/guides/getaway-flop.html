How to play

GETAWAY FLOP is a heist race in the style of a 1930s rubber-hose cartoon, with bendy arms and legs, ink hatching, film grain, flicker and scratches. Four crooks race across the city rooftops to the getaway car. The first crook to reach it scores a getaway, and the first to three getaways wins the match.

Player 1 hops left and right with A and D. Tap again in mid-air for a second hop. W, S or Space fires whatever you are holding. Player 2 hops with Left and Right and fires with Up or Down. On a phone there are on-screen hop buttons and a BANG button. Esc opens the menu.

Your crook is wobbly. The way your body tilts steers each hop and points your gun, and if you land tilted too far you flop flat on the roof.

Tips and strategy

Hop small. Short, steady hops keep your body upright. Big panicky hops tilt you over and end in a flop.

Use the double hop for gaps. Save the mid-air second hop for wide gaps between buildings or to correct a hop that is falling short.

Arm up. Pick up the weapons on the roofs: the POPGUN, the BLUNDERBUSS and the CREAM PIE. Knocking the leader off a roof just before the car can swing a whole round.

Aim with your tilt. Your gun points the way your body leans, so line yourself up before you fire.

What's in it

A single-player campaign of heists against three bots, where the bots get sharper as you go and you unlock hats. There is also a two-player mode on one keyboard, with two bots joining in. Each match is first to three getaways, with an end card at the finish.

The art is drawn like an old cartoon: three-tone shading with ink hatching in the shadows, thick ink outlines, a two-colour film grade, grain, flicker, scratches and an iris wipe between rounds.

Questions

Do I need an account? No. Your campaign progress and hats are saved in your browser.

Can two people play? Yes, on one keyboard, against two bots.

Is it violent? It is old-cartoon slapstick. Crooks are knocked off roofs with popguns and pies; there is no blood.

Does it flash? The picture has a constant gentle old-film flicker, but no bright flashes.
