How to play

A block of fifty-five aliens, eleven across and five rows deep, marches from side to side above you. Each time the block reaches an edge it steps down. You steer a cannon along the ground and shoot upwards. Only one of your shots can be on screen at a time. Aliens in the top row are worth 30 points, the next two rows 20 and the bottom two rows 10. Clearing a wave adds 100 points and brings in the next one.

The aliens drop bombs back at you, but only the lowest alien in each column can fire. You start with three cannons, and a bomb that hits you costs one. The bombs and your shot are cleared when you are hit, so you get a clean restart. The run ends when your last cannon is gone, or straight away if any alien gets down to the ground. Four green bunkers stand between you and them. Every hit, from either side, bites a chunk out of them, and they are not rebuilt between waves.

This is the game Taito released in 1978 as Space Invaders, designed by Tomohiro Nishikado. As in the original, the aliens speed up as their numbers fall. A full block takes about 0.7 seconds per step. The last one left steps about ten times as often.

Tips and strategy

  • The march speeds up as the block shrinks, so the final few aliens are the dangerous part of a wave. Keep some shots for them, rather than chasing the saucer late on.
  • The block steps down only when its outermost living column reaches a wall. Clearing whole columns at the edges means it travels further between steps down. That buys time before it reaches the ground.
  • Each wave starts lower than the last, by one step for each of the first four waves. Each one also marches 10% faster than the one before and drops more and faster bombs.
  • Your shot can meet a falling bomb and cancel it. If you have no clear escape from a bomb, firing straight into it can save the cannon.
  • Your own shots wear the bunkers from underneath. Firing from behind a bunker cuts a hole through it, which is useful as a firing slot but takes away your cover.
  • The saucer crosses the top of the screen roughly every 16 to 30 seconds, worth 50, 100, 150 or 300 at random. It flies at a steady speed and covers about two of its own widths while your shot climbs, so fire well ahead of it.
  • Holding fire shoots again the moment your last shot is gone. A shot that hits a low alien comes back sooner than one fired into empty sky, so close targets keep your fire rate up.

What's in it

One mode with endless waves, three cannons and a top-ten board for score. Three alien designs, a saucer, two kinds of bomb (straight and zig-zag, both equally deadly) and four bunkers that wear away. Move with the arrow keys or A D, or put the cannon under the mouse. Fire with Space or a click. On a phone the cannon follows your finger and fires while you touch the screen.

Questions

Why can't I fire faster?

Only one shot can be on screen at a time. You can fire again only when the last shot has hit something or left the screen.

Do the bunkers come back?

No. They are built once at the start of a run and wear away over every wave that follows.

How many lives do I get?

Three. The cannons left in reserve are shown in the bar at the top. No extra lives are awarded.

What is the saucer worth?

50, 100, 150 or 300 points, chosen at random each time it appears.
