How to play

Each side has a bat, and there is one ball between them. If the ball gets past your bat, the other side wins the point. The first to eleven wins the match, and there is no need to win by two. After each point the ball goes back to the centre, waits just under a second while it flashes, and is then served towards the player who just lost the point. The ball bounces off the top and bottom edges of the court, but nothing stops it at the ends.

Where the ball hits the bat decides where it goes. A hit in the middle sends it straight back. A hit near either end sends it off at an angle of up to about 53 degrees. If your bat is moving when it meets the ball, some of that movement is added to the ball, so you can cut it up or down the court. The ball also gets faster with every hit in a rally, and goes back to serving speed when a point is scored.

Bat-and-ball games go back to the 1970s. Pong, released by Atari in 1972, was the one that started the arcade business.

Tips and strategy

  • The computer chooses where to aim once, as the ball turns towards it, and adds an error to that choice. A sharp angle off the end of your bat gives that error the most room to matter.
  • The computer also waits before it moves: about a quarter of a second on Easy, a sixth on Fair and under a tenth on Hard. A fast, angled return hit early uses up that wait.
  • Once it has hit the ball, the computer drifts back towards the middle at about a third of its full speed. A quick return wide to the side it has just left makes it cover the most ground.
  • The ball speeds up with each hit and stops getting faster after twenty hits, at roughly twice its serving speed. On harder difficulties every hit leaves the bat a little faster as well.
  • The mouse moves your bat to the pointer at once, which makes big angle changes easy. The keys move the bat at a steady speed, which gives you control over the cut. A held key always wins. When no key is held, moving the mouse puts the bat under the pointer.
  • To return the ball straight and safely, meet it with the middle third of the bat and keep the bat still.

What's in it

Four buttons on the home screen. Easy, Fair and Hard are one-player matches against the computer, which gets quicker to react, more accurate and faster across the court at each step up. Two players shares one keyboard, with W S on the left against ↑ ↓ on the right. In a one-player game you can also use the arrow keys or the mouse. P pauses and R goes back to the home screen.

Questions

What does the leaderboard measure?

The margin of a one-player win, plus a bonus for difficulty. Your winning margin is multiplied by ten, and 40 points are added on Fair or 80 on Hard. An 11–2 win on Hard therefore scores 170.

Do losses or two-player games count?

No. Only one-player wins go on the board.

Is the computer beatable on Hard?

Yes. On Hard it still aims with a small error and reacts after a short delay. A well-angled ball from the end of the bat can still beat it.

Can I play with a touchscreen?

Yes. In a one-player game, hold your finger on the screen and the left bat follows it.
