For parents

What your child will find on PLAYPILE, game by game, and how the site works behind the scenes.

The short version

  • Every game is free and plays in the browser. Nothing to download, nothing to buy, no in-game purchases.
  • No account is needed for anything, and the site never asks for a name, age or email to play.
  • There is no chat and no way to message other players.
  • Two games, HOOPS and INKVALE, can be played online with a friend who has been given a four-letter room code. Nobody can join without that code.
  • The site shows adverts from Google. They are never placed over a game while it is being played.
  • PLAYPILE is made for a general audience, not specifically for children under 13. Some games contain violence, listed below.

What is in each game

Written by the person who made them, to describe what is actually on screen.

KICKBACK

DUNGEON QUEST

Action. A crime story seen from above: robberies, police chases and gunfights with the police. Small figures, no blood close up.

Sport. Skateboarding in the street. Falls, but nothing violent.

SOUTHPAW

Sport. Cartoon pixel-art boxing, plus wrestling and sumo. Fighters are knocked down; no blood.

Strategy. Storybook fantasy battles: archers, knights and wizards against ink monsters, which splash into ink when beaten. No blood.

PENGUIN DASH

Arcade. Cartoon food falls and stacks up. A seagull steals bits of burger, and customers gag and walk out when you catch a boot, a fish or a sock. No violence.

WRECKING BALL

Arcade. Cartoon buildings, cars and gas tanks get smashed, crumple and explode into dust and fireballs. There are no people in it and nobody gets hurt.

MOWER MADNESS

Driving. Cartoon garden chaos: gnomes and toys get knocked flying and shatter, a dog chases the mower and bumps into it, and cats leap away hissing. No animals are hurt and there is no blood.

SUSHI CONVEYOR

Arcade. Cartoon customers in a sushi bar get grumpy and storm out if kept waiting, and a cat steals plates. No violence.

BOMB SQUAD

Puzzle. A cartoon bomb that goes BOOM with a comic-book explosion, a bright white flash and a sooty pair of eyes when you fail. A strike tints the screen red and shakes it. Nobody is hurt and there is no blood.

GRAPPLE GOBLIN

Arcade. A cartoon goblin swings over spikes, lava and icy water and dodges bats. A fall is a puff of smoke and a restart at the last flag. Nothing graphic.

PARRY

Arcade. Cartoon fantasy fighters (bandits, knights, ninjas, ogres, pirates) are knocked flying or puff away with a KO when you block them back. Small cartoon explosions from bombs and fireballs. No blood.

ONE TILE

Puzzle. A cartoon pixel thief sneaks past guards, cameras and lasers. Being spotted sets off an alarm and a soft red pulse over the room, then the level restarts. Nobody is hurt.

DEMOLITION DERBY

Driving. Cartoon top-down cars ram each other, lose panels, smoke and catch fire. There are no people shown and nobody is hurt.

TOUGE DRIFT

Driving. Cartoon cars racing and drifting on a mountain road. They scrape the guardrail and bump each other, with sparks and tyre smoke. No crashes that hurt anyone and no people shown.

BLADE DASH

Action. Stylised ink stick-figure samurai fights: enemies split in two when cut, barrels explode, and hits leave red ink splats on the paper. No realistic blood or gore.

TINY HARBOR

Adventure. A peaceful island game. Nobody gets hurt and you cannot die. You chop trees and catch, cook and eat fish; storms bring rain, thunder and lightning.

STACK

Arcade. A stacking game. Nothing of concern.

DRIFTER

Arcade. A spaceship shooting at asteroids. Nothing of concern.

PIPEWORKS

Puzzle. A pipe puzzle. Nothing of concern.

SWEEP

Puzzle. Minesweeper. Nothing of concern.

QUICKDRAW

Shooter. Cartoon western gunfights between two cowboys, with hits counted by limb. Bright muzzle flashes; no blood.

HOOPS

Sport. Basketball. Can be played online with a friend using a room code; there is no chat.

SPIKE

Sport. Volleyball between two jelly blobs. Nothing of concern.

CROSSING

Arcade. A pixel-art frog crossing roads and rivers. The frog can be run over or fall in the water, cartoon style.

Privacy and children

Scores and progress are kept in the browser on your own device. A child can play every game without an account. If an account was made by a child under 13, email us and it will be deleted. You can see and delete everything the site has saved in a browser on the privacy settings page, and choose non-personalised adverts there too.

Screen time and wellbeing

Every game can be paused, and none of them punish you for stopping. None use daily login rewards, timers that make you come back, or anything to buy. Some games have bright flashes; the accessibility page lists them.
